What Is This?

You message @LittleBitsOfBuddhaBot on Telegram. A teacher responds — drawing from 286 suttas in the Pali Canon (Bhikkhu Sujato's translations, CC0), remembering your past conversations, adapting its tone to your practice level. It's grounded in the Early Buddhist Texts — the oldest stratum of the Buddha's teaching as preserved in the Theravāda tradition.

It's not a generic chatbot with a Buddhist prompt. It searches by meaning, cites its sources, remembers where you left off, guides meditation, tracks your practice, and grows with you.

A Conversation

You
I keep getting angry at work. Nothing helps.
The Buddha
Anger is like picking up a hot coal to throw at someone else — you are the one who gets burned. (SN7.2, Akkosa Sutta)

When anger arises, what feeling precedes it? Often there is a wound — an expectation unmet, a boundary crossed. The anger is the second arrow. The first was the event. The second is your response to it.

The Sallatha Sutta (SN36.6) teaches this directly: the uninstructed person, when touched by pain, feels two pains — the physical and the mental. The instructed person feels only one.
You
I've been sitting with that. I notice the tightness before the anger now.
The Buddha
That noticing is the practice. The awareness precedes the choice — and in that gap, freedom lives. This is sati in its most direct form. Not as a technique, but as a way of being present to what arises without adding to it.

The Learning Path

The bot doesn't just answer questions — it gently guides you through the core teachings of Early Buddhism. The structured learning path adapts to what you're exploring naturally:

🌱
Four Noble Truths
The foundation: suffering, its origin, its cessation, and the path. The bot detects when you're exploring these themes and weaves in relevant suttas.
🛤️
Noble Eightfold Path
Right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, concentration. Each factor explored through your own questions and practice.
🧘
Meditation Practice
Breathing meditation (ānāpānasati) and loving-kindness (mettā). Guided sessions you can do right in the chat. The bot tracks your consistency.
📖
Sutta Study
When you're ready, the teacher points you to specific suttas. Not assigned reading — discoveries that arise naturally from your questions.

What's Built

Phase 1 — Memory & Search Complete
The bot that remembers
  • Dapr state store for per-user conversation history
  • 286 suttas from SuttaCentral embedded as vectors in Redis
  • RAG pipeline: semantic search → context injection → LLM
  • Redis-cell rate limiting per user
Phase 2 — Actors & Personality Complete
The bot that knows you
  • Dapr Virtual Actors: one actor per seeker, stateful lifecycle
  • Practice level detection from conversation patterns
  • Adaptive system prompts: newcomer → beginner → intermediate → experienced
  • Wisdom Service: LLM + RAG + Dapr Conversation API
  • Telegram commands: /start, /level, /forget (GDPR), /help
Phase 3 — Guided Practice Complete
The bot that teaches
  • Guided meditations: breathing + mettā via Dapr Workflows (durable, survives restarts)
  • Daily sutta delivery + weekly practice check-ins via Dapr Jobs
  • Structured learning paths: Four Noble Truths → Eightfold Path → specific practices
  • Tool calling: LLM autonomously searches suttas when your question needs it
  • Semantic response caching (LangCache): "What is dukkha?" and "explain suffering" hit the same cache
  • Practice journaling: /sit to log, /journal to review, weekly summaries
Phase 4 — Community & Depth Future
The bot that grows
  • Long-term seeker memory across months (Redis Agent Memory Server)
  • Anonymous practice analytics — patterns across the sangha (Redis TimeSeries)
  • Hybrid search: full-text + vector for richer sutta retrieval
  • Full Tipiṭaka: expand from 286 to 5,000+ suttas with int8 quantization
  • Group practice sessions — sit together, asynchronously
  • Retreat mode — intensive multi-day guided programs
"Why not just use one container with SQLite?"
Because a single process can't: vector-search 286 suttas by meaning in milliseconds (Redis), manage thousands of concurrent seeker sessions with automatic lifecycle (Dapr Actors), run durable 15-minute guided meditations that survive restarts (Dapr Workflows), cache semantically similar LLM calls to cut costs 60% (LangCache), scrub personal information before it reaches the LLM (Dapr Conversation API), or schedule personalized daily practice prompts per user (Dapr Jobs). The architecture stops being a showcase when every component is load-bearing.
"Why Early Buddhist Texts specifically?"
The EBTs — primarily the Pali Nikāyas and their Chinese parallels — represent the oldest recoverable stratum of the Buddha's teaching. They're the foundation that all Buddhist traditions build on. By grounding the bot in these texts (286 suttas from SuttaCentral, translated by Bhikkhu Sujato, released under CC0), every response traces back to a specific, verifiable source. No syncretism, no hallucinated references, no "the Buddha once said" without a citation. The teacher speaks from the texts, not about them.
